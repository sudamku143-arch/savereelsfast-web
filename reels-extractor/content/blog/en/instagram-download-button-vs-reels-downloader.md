Instagram now lets you download some Reels straight from the app. So why would anyone use a separate downloader? The honest answer is that both have a place. This guide compares them side by side, so you can pick the right one for each Reel.

## How Instagram's built-in download works

On many public Reels, the **Share** sheet includes a **Download** option. Tap it and the Reel is saved to your phone's camera roll, without leaving the app. It has been rolling out gradually, so whether you see it depends on your region, the account type and the creator's settings.

That last part matters: creators can turn downloads off for their Reels, and some Reels don't offer the option at all. Reels from private accounts never do.

## What the built-in download adds

A Reel saved with Instagram's own button carries an **Instagram watermark** with the creator's username. That is deliberate: it keeps the credit attached wherever the video travels. For sharing a clip with a friend, that's a good thing.

It's less helpful when you want the plain video, for example to keep a clean copy of your own Reel, to review footage frame by frame, or to use a clip you have permission to edit.

## What a downloader gives you

A browser-based tool like our [Instagram video downloader](/instagram-video-downloader) saves the file Instagram uses for playback: no watermark added, no re-encoding, in the best quality Instagram serves for that Reel, up to 1080p. It also works on a computer, where the app has no download option, and it can save the sound on its own when Instagram provides a separate audio track.

It has the same hard limit as the app: only public Reels. It never logs in to Instagram, so private accounts stay private.

## When to use Instagram's button

Use the built-in option when it's there and you're sharing the Reel with someone else. The watermark credits the creator automatically, it saves straight to your camera roll, and it respects the creator's choice: if they turned downloads off, that's a signal they'd rather you didn't.

## When a downloader makes more sense

A downloader is the practical choice when you need a clean file of your own Reel, when you're on a computer, when you only want the audio, or when you have the creator's permission to use a clip in an edit. It's also handy when the Download option simply doesn't appear on your account or in your country.

## Respect the creator either way

A creator turning off downloads doesn't make their Reel private, and a downloader can still fetch a public video. That doesn't mean you should ignore their wishes. Saving a Reel to watch offline is generally fine; re-uploading it, especially without the watermark, is not okay without permission and can break copyright law and Instagram's terms. When in doubt, ask, and always credit them.

## Quick answers

**Does a downloader remove Instagram's watermark?** No. It doesn't edit videos at all. It saves the file Instagram uses for playback, which never had the watermark in the first place; the watermark is only added when you use Instagram's own Download button.

**Does the creator get notified?** A downloader only reads the public post, the same way a logged-out visitor would, so nothing is sent to the creator.

**Which one gives better quality?** Both start from the same upload, so neither can be sharper than the original. The downloader's copy is simply the file without anything added on top.
