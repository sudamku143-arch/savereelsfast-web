X moves fast, and its videos travel faster: a funny clip, a news moment or a useful tip can be everywhere within hours. Sharing them is part of the fun. But reposting a video is not always the same thing as re-uploading it, and the difference decides whether you are on safe ground. This guide explains how to share X videos fairly and legally. It is general information, not legal advice.

## Repost, quote or re-upload: three different things

**Repost or quote.** Using X's own repost and quote features shares the original post, with the creator's name and link attached. The video stays on X, and the creator keeps the credit and the views. This is what the feature is for, and it is the safest way to share.

**Embed or link.** Putting a link to the post, or embedding it on a website, also points back to the original. It respects the creator and the platform's rules.

**Download and re-upload.** Saving a video and uploading it as your own post, or on another platform, copies the work. This is where copyright problems start.

## Who owns a video on X

In most cases the creator owns the video, or another company does. A video being public and easy to watch does not mean it is free to reuse. Copyright usually exists from the moment the video is made, and that is true whether or not there is a watermark or a notice.

## When downloading is fine

There are reasonable, low-risk reasons to save a video:

- **Your own videos,** as a backup of something you posted.
- **Personal viewing,** such as watching offline or keeping a tutorial you follow.
- **Content you have permission to use,** ideally in writing.
- **Content under an open licence,** such as Creative Commons, used as the licence allows.

## If you want to reuse someone's video

Follow a simple routine:

1. **Ask.** Reply or send a message to the creator and describe how you plan to use the clip.
2. **Keep the answer.** Save the message where you can find it.
3. **Credit them.** Name the creator and link to the original post.
4. **Do not edit it misleadingly.** Cropping out a name or changing the meaning is not acceptable.
5. **Remove it if asked.**

Some creators are happy to share, and many will say yes if you ask kindly. Brands and news organisations often have formal request processes.

## Fair use is not a shortcut

Some countries have limited exceptions, such as fair use in the United States, for uses like commentary, criticism, news reporting or education. Whether a particular use qualifies depends on several factors, and it is decided case by case. It is not a rule that lets you copy a video as long as you add a caption. If your use is commercial or the video is central to your content, ask for permission or get advice.

## Protect yourself and your account

Reposting without permission can lead to takedown requests, a warning on your account or worse. A takedown is easier to avoid than to fix. If a creator asks you to remove a video, do it promptly and politely. If you are the creator, you can make it easy for people to share your work correctly. Put your handle on the video, write a clear caption and say how you want it credited.
