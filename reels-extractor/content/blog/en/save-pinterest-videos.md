Pinterest is where ideas go to be collected: recipes, room makeovers, workouts, craft tutorials. But when you are in a kitchen with a weak signal, or at a workshop with no Wi-Fi, a saved pin is only useful if you can play it. This guide explains the difference between saving a pin and saving a video, and how to keep a public Pinterest video as a file.

## What Pinterest's Save button does

The **Save** button puts a pin on one of your boards. That keeps it organised and easy to find, and Pinterest may suggest similar ideas because of it. But the video itself stays on Pinterest. To play it, you need a connection, and if the creator removes the pin, it is gone from your board too.

That is why people who want a reliable copy need a video file.

## Why keep a video file

There are good reasons to want an offline copy:

- **Following a tutorial** where you want to pause and replay without a signal.
- **Building a mood board** in a design tool or presentation.
- **Keeping your own pins** as a backup.
- **Sharing with a client or team** who do not use Pinterest, when you have the right to do so.

## Saving a public Pinterest video

For a public video pin, a browser tool can save an MP4:

1. Open the pin and choose **Share**, then **Copy link**. You can also copy the address from the browser bar.
2. Open our [Pinterest video downloader](/pinterest-video-downloader).
3. Paste the link. The Pinterest tab is selected for you. Short pin.it links are accepted.
4. Check the preview and choose **Download**.

No account is needed, and you should never enter your Pinterest password into a downloader.

## Which pins work

Video pins that are public work. Pins that contain only an image do not, and the tool will tell you there is no video rather than return the picture. Some pins link to a video hosted elsewhere, such as a YouTube video. In that case, copy the link to the video's original page and use the matching tool, for instance our [YouTube video downloader](/youtube-video-downloader).

If a link fails, try it in a private browser window. If the pin does not show there, it is not public.

## Quality and format

Pinterest videos are often vertical, and a saved copy will play on phones, tablets and computers as an MP4. Quality depends on what Pinterest serves. For your own pins, the original you exported from your editor is always the best copy. For help choosing a format, see [MP4, WebM and MOV compared](/blog/best-video-formats-mp4-vs-webm-vs-mov).

## Using saved pins responsibly

Pinterest encourages saving and sharing pins on its own platform, which is different from copying a video elsewhere. A few sensible rules apply:

- **Personal reference is fine.** Watching a tutorial or planning a project is a normal use.
- **Ask before reusing.** Using someone's video in your own content, a shop or an advertisement needs permission.
- **Credit and link.** Name the creator and link to the original pin whenever you share.
- **Remove on request.** If the creator asks, take it down.

Read more in our guide to [downloading videos safely and legally](/blog/download-social-media-videos-safely-and-legally).

## Organising your saved videos

Once you have files, a little structure helps:

1. **Name them clearly,** such as "sourdough-tutorial-creator-name.mp4".
2. **Use folders** by project or topic.
3. **Note the source** in a text file so you can credit the creator later.
4. **Back them up** to cloud storage.

## Troubleshooting

- **"No video found":** the pin is an image. Look for a video pin.
- **"Private or login required":** the pin is on a private board or was removed.
- **A pin.it link fails:** open it in your browser first and copy the full address.
- **A busy message:** wait a moment and try again.
