Saving a video to watch offline is one of the most common things people do online, and one of the easiest to get wrong. Some downloader sites are unsafe, and the rules about what you may do with a file are not always obvious. This guide covers both sides: how to stay safe, and how to stay on the right side of copyright.

*This article is general information, not legal advice. Laws differ from country to country, so check the rules where you live if it matters.*

## Part 1: Download safely

### Choose tools that need nothing installed

The safest downloader is a web page that works in your browser. Be cautious with programs and apps from unknown publishers, especially ones that ask you to install a browser extension or turn off your antivirus. If a site tells you to download a ".exe" or ".apk" file just to save a video, close it.

### Never share your social media login

A genuine downloader for public videos only needs the link. If a site asks for your Instagram, TikTok or YouTube password, or asks you to "log in with" the platform, it may be trying to take over your account. Close the page and change your password if you already entered it.

### Check the address and the connection

Look for HTTPS and a padlock in the address bar, and read the web address carefully. Copycat sites often use slightly altered names. Type the address yourself or use a bookmark instead of following a link in an advert or a message from a stranger.

### Watch out for fake buttons and pop-ups

Many free sites are crowded with adverts that look like download buttons. The real button usually sits directly beneath the video preview. If a click opens a new tab, asks you to allow notifications or claims your device is infected, close it without following any of the instructions.

## Part 2: Download legally

### Public does not mean free to reuse

A video being visible to everyone does not give you the right to copy it, edit it or upload it somewhere else. Copyright normally belongs to the creator from the moment they make the video, whether or not it shows a copyright symbol.

### Personal use versus sharing

Saving a clip to watch privately is very different from posting it on your own channel. Some countries allow limited private copying or have "fair use" or "fair dealing" exceptions, but these depend on the purpose, how much you copy and the effect on the creator. Re-uploading someone else's video as your own is rarely acceptable and can lead to takedowns or account penalties.

### Your own content is the safest case

Downloading videos you made yourself, such as a backup of a Reel or a clip you posted years ago, is the clearest use. You already own the rights, and you can reuse the file on other platforms or keep it in an archive.

### Get permission or look for open licences

If you want to use someone else's video, ask the creator and keep the reply. Some creators publish under open licences such as Creative Commons, which state exactly how you may reuse the work and how to give credit. Follow the licence terms every time.

### Respect platform rules and privacy

Each platform has its own terms of service, and they can restrict downloading or reposting. Never try to access private or friends-only videos, and be careful with videos that show other people, especially children.

## A quick safety and legality checklist

- The tool works in the browser and asks only for a public link.
- I am not entering a password anywhere.
- The site uses HTTPS and I typed or bookmarked the address.
- The file is a normal video file, not a program.
- The video is mine, I have permission, or the licence allows my use.
- I will credit the creator where appropriate and remove content on request.
