Pinterest is not a typical social feed. People go there to plan things: a recipe for the weekend, a room to redecorate, an outfit for a trip. That means a good video pin can keep finding viewers long after you posted it. This guide walks through how video pins work and how to make ones that get found.

Pinterest updates its formats and specifications from time to time, so check its current creator guidance before you plan around exact numbers.

## Why Pinterest video is different

On most platforms, a video is popular for a few days and then fades. On Pinterest, people search for ideas, so pins are found through keywords, boards and recommendations for months or longer. A helpful pin can bring steady visitors, and that makes Pinterest a strong place for evergreen how-to and inspiration content.

## What makes a good video pin

The pins that do well tend to be **useful, clear and inspiring**:

- **Vertical format.** Tall videos take up more space on a phone. A 9:16 ratio is a common choice.
- **A strong first second.** Show the result or the key step immediately.
- **Text on screen.** Many people browse without sound, so use short, readable captions.
- **Steps over speeches.** Show how to do something in a few quick stages.
- **A clear point.** One idea per video works better than several.

Good topics include recipes, home projects, style ideas, travel plans, crafts and short tutorials.

## Titles, descriptions and keywords

Because Pinterest works like a search engine, words matter. Write a title that says what the pin offers, such as "Easy 15-minute pasta for busy weeknights". In the description, add a couple of natural sentences that use the phrases people search for. Avoid stuffing keywords, since a plain, helpful description is better for viewers and for search.

Add relevant topics and choose the board carefully. A board about "Small kitchen ideas" tells Pinterest what your pin is about.

## Boards and consistency

Boards organise your pins and help Pinterest understand your topics. Create boards with clear names, save new pins to the right one and keep each board focused. Posting steadily, even a few pins a week, tends to work better than uploading a big batch and disappearing.

## Sending viewers to your site

If you have a blog, shop or channel, link the pin to a matching page, not to your home page. A recipe pin should lead to that recipe. Make sure the page loads quickly on a phone, since most Pinterest visitors are browsing on one.

## Measuring what works

Pinterest's analytics show how many people saw, saved and clicked your pins. Saves are especially valuable, since they show that people want to come back. Look for patterns: which topics, hooks and lengths do people save? Make more of those and adjust the rest.

## Using other people's pins

Pinterest is built on saving and sharing, and saving a pin to your own board is how it is meant to be used. Copying someone's video and uploading it as your own is a different matter, and can cause copyright problems. Credit creators, link back to the source and ask permission before you reuse. ## Common mistakes

- **Horizontal videos** that are small on a phone.
- **No text on screen** for people watching silently.
- **Vague titles** that give search nothing to work with.
- **Sending traffic to a home page** instead of the relevant page.
- **Giving up early.** Pinterest results often build over weeks.
