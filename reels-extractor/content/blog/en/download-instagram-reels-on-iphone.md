Most guides for saving Reels on an iPhone start with "install this app". You don't need one. Safari can download a video file on its own, and the Photos app can take it from there. This guide walks through the whole route, from copying the link to finding the Reel in your camera roll.

## Before you start

You need three things: an iPhone on a recent version of iOS, Safari (or Chrome), and a Reel from a public account. Private accounts can't be downloaded by any honest tool, because their videos are only shown to approved followers. If you can't open the Reel while logged out of Instagram, a downloader can't reach it either.

It also helps to decide why you are saving it. Keeping a recipe, a workout or your own Reel for offline viewing is the normal case. Re-uploading someone else's Reel as your own is a different matter, and we cover that at the end.

## Step 1: Copy the Reel's link

Open the Reel in the Instagram app and tap the **Share** icon (the paper plane). In the sheet that appears, tap **Copy link**. The link lands on your clipboard and looks like instagram.com/reel/ followed by a short code.

Links from the Share sheet are the most reliable. Copying an address by hand, or from a screenshot, often leaves out part of the code.

## Step 2: Paste it into the downloader in Safari

Open Safari and go to our [Instagram video downloader](/instagram-video-downloader). Tap **Paste**, or long-press the box and choose Paste. The Instagram tab is picked for you, and the tool fetches the Reel straight away: you will see a preview with the thumbnail and the creator's name.

Tap **Download Video**. Safari asks whether you want to download the file; tap **Download**. A small arrow appears in the address bar while it saves.

## Step 3: Move the video into Photos

Safari saves downloads to the Files app, not to Photos, which is where most people get stuck. To move it:

1. Tap the **downloads arrow** in Safari's address bar and tap the video.
2. The video opens in a preview. Tap the **Share** icon.
3. Choose **Save Video**.

The Reel now sits in your camera roll like any other clip. You can also find the original file later in the Files app, under Downloads.

## Using Chrome instead of Safari

Chrome on iPhone works too. After tapping Download Video, Chrome shows a bar at the bottom with an **Open in** option. Open the file, tap Share, then **Save Video**. If you miss the bar, Chrome keeps its downloads in the Files app, in a folder named Chrome.

## Put the downloader on your Home Screen

If you save Reels often, add the site to your Home Screen so it opens like an app. In Safari, tap **Share**, scroll down and tap **Add to Home Screen**. It takes up almost no space, needs no App Store install and never asks for your Instagram password.

## Why not just screen-record?

Screen recording works, but it captures everything: the progress bar, notifications, your volume indicator and a lower-quality copy of the video. A downloader fetches the same file Instagram itself plays, so the result is cleaner and usually sharper, and there is no watermark added on top.

## What if the sound is missing?

Most Reels download with sound. A few are posted silently, and Instagram sometimes delivers the picture and the sound as separate files. When that happens the result shows a separate **Download Audio** button, which saves the sound as an M4A file. Our [guide to downloading Reel audio](/blog/download-instagram-reel-audio) explains what you get.

## If the download fails

The usual causes are a private account, a deleted Reel or a link that points to a profile instead of a Reel. Instagram also limits requests for short periods, so waiting a minute and pasting the link again often fixes it. Our [troubleshooting guide](/blog/instagram-reel-not-downloading) goes through each case.

## Use saved Reels fairly

Saving a public Reel to watch later is generally fine. Posting it again on your own account without the creator's permission can break copyright law and Instagram's terms. If you share it, ask first and credit the creator.
