You paste a Facebook link into a downloader and it says the video is private. Or a friend's video plays perfectly for you but will not download. Nothing is broken. Facebook lets people choose who sees each video, and those choices decide what any tool can reach. This guide explains the audiences, how to tell them apart and what that means for saving a video.

## The audience setting on every video

Every Facebook post carries an audience setting chosen by whoever posted it. The main ones are:

- **Public:** anyone, on or off Facebook, can watch it.
- **Friends:** only people on the poster's friends list.
- **Friends except or specific friends:** a smaller, hand-picked group.
- **Only me:** visible to the poster alone.
- **Groups and events:** visible to members, and in private groups, only to members.

Facebook enforces these settings on its servers. A downloader that does not log in can only reach what a stranger could see, which means public videos.

## Why a video plays for you but will not download

If you are logged in and the video is shared with friends, you can watch it because Facebook recognises you. A downloader has no such access, and it should not, because it would mean handing over your account. The video is not "protected" in a technical sense. It is simply not meant for people outside that audience.

## How to check a link before you try

You can predict the result in a few seconds:

1. Copy the link to the video.
2. Open a private or incognito browser window, where you are not logged in.
3. Paste the link and see whether the video plays.

If it plays, it is public, and a browser tool such as our [Facebook video downloader](/facebook-video-downloader) can usually save it. If Facebook asks you to log in, the video is not public, and no tool without your login can fetch it.

## Pages, Reels and Watch

Videos posted by Pages, businesses and public figures are often public, as are many Reels and Watch videos. Videos on personal profiles are more often friends-only. The small icon next to the date on a post shows the audience: a globe means public, and two silhouettes mean friends.

## What about private groups?

Content in a private group belongs to that community, and the members expect it to stay there. Trying to get around that is a bad idea technically and a breach of trust. If you need something from a group, ask the person who posted it to share it with you, or to send you the original file.

## Saving your own videos, whatever the audience

You can always keep videos you posted, even the private ones, because they are yours. Use your video settings, Meta's tools for pages and creators, or the "download your information" feature in your account. That is the reliable way to back up family clips that were never public.

## Respect the person behind the video

Being able to save a public video does not mean you may reuse it however you like. A public video is still owned by its creator. Save it to watch privately, and ask permission before you post it elsewhere. ## If you post videos yourself

Think about the audience before you upload. A public video can be watched, shared and saved by anyone, so choose **Friends** or a smaller group for anything personal. You can change the audience later from the post's menu. If you are a creator who wants reach, public is the right choice, and keeping your original files protects you either way.

## Quick answers

- **A private video will not download:** correct, and that is how it should work.
- **A public video will not download:** copy the link from the video itself, and try again in a minute.
- **A live video:** wait until the recording is published.
- **Someone else's friends-only video:** ask them to share it with you.
