Snapchat is more than private messages. Two of its main video features, Spotlight and Stories, are often mixed up, but they do different jobs and reach different audiences. Choosing the right one can change who sees your video and how long it lasts. This guide explains both and helps you decide.

Snapchat updates its features and rules from time to time, so check the app for the latest details.

## What Snapchat Stories are

A **Story** is a collection of snaps that friends or followers can watch. Traditionally, a Story disappears after 24 hours, which suits casual, in-the-moment sharing: what you are doing, where you are, a quick reaction. You choose who can see it, from close friends to everyone who follows you. You can also keep a Story longer by saving it, for instance to your Memories or a profile highlight, depending on your settings.

Stories are best for **staying in touch** and for creators who want to keep a community close.

## What Spotlight is

**Spotlight** is Snapchat's public feed of short videos, similar in spirit to other short-video platforms. Videos in Spotlight are shown to people who do not follow you, based on what Snapchat thinks they will enjoy. Clips are short, usually up to about a minute, and vertical.

Spotlight is best for **reaching new people** and for videos that are entertaining, useful or creative on their own.

## Side by side

| | Stories | Spotlight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audience | Friends and followers you choose | Anyone, through recommendations |
| Lifespan | Usually 24 hours, unless saved | Stays available as a public video |
| Best for | Everyday moments and community | Polished short videos aimed at discovery |
| Feel | Casual, personal | More like a public video feed |

## Choosing between them

Ask yourself what you want from the video:

- **A quick update for people who already know you?** Post it to your Story.
- **A clip that would make sense to a stranger?** Post it to Spotlight, with a clear idea and a strong first second.
- **Both?** Many creators use Stories to tease and Spotlight to reach a wider audience.

## Making a good Spotlight video

Videos that do well in short-video feeds usually share a few traits: a hook in the first second, a single clear idea, vertical framing, on-screen text for viewers without sound and a satisfying ending. Add relevant topics and keep the caption short. Check the numbers Snapchat gives you and repeat what people watch to the end.

Creator programmes and rewards on Snapchat have changed over time, so read the current terms before you plan around income.

## Privacy comes first

Snapchat's culture is built on the idea that private snaps stay private. Do not try to save or share someone's private snap, and never give your Snapchat login to a third-party site. If you post publicly, remember that a public Spotlight video can be watched and shared by anyone.
