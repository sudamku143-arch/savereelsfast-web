If you have ever tried to send a video that would not play, or wondered why a clip from your phone looks different in an editing app, you have run into video formats. The three names you will see most often are MP4, WebM and MOV. This guide explains what they are, how they differ and which one to pick for each job.

## Container versus codec: the one idea that clears it all up

A video file has two parts. The **codec** is the method used to compress the picture and the sound, such as H.264, HEVC (H.265), VP9 or AV1. The **container** is the file type that holds the compressed video and audio together, and that is what MP4, WebM and MOV really are.

That is why two MP4 files can behave differently: one might use H.264 and play on almost anything, while another uses a newer codec that older devices cannot decode. When a video will not play, the codec is usually the cause, not the container.

## MP4: the safe default

MP4 (file ending .mp4) is the most widely supported video format. It plays on phones, computers, tablets, televisions, game consoles and in every major browser and social app. It most often carries H.264 video with AAC audio, a combination that has been supported almost everywhere for many years.

**Choose MP4 when** you want a video to work for the largest number of people, for uploads to social platforms, for email and messaging, and for saving a clip to watch offline. It balances quality and file size well, which is why it is the format [SaveReelsFast](/) saves videos in.

## WebM: built for the web

WebM (file ending .webm) is an open format designed for the web. It usually holds VP8, VP9 or AV1 video with Opus or Vorbis audio. It compresses efficiently, so files can be smaller at the same visual quality, and it supports transparency in some cases.

The catch is compatibility. WebM plays in most modern browsers, but support on some older phones, televisions and editing programs is patchy. Some platforms accept it for uploads, while many social apps prefer MP4.

**Choose WebM when** you publish video on your own website and want small files, or when you need a short animation with a transparent background for a web page.

## MOV: the editor's friend

MOV (file ending .mov) is Apple's QuickTime format. Recordings from iPhones and Macs often arrive as MOV, and video professionals like it because it can hold high-quality codecs such as ProRes, which keep more detail for editing and colour work.

Those high-quality files are large, and not every device plays them smoothly. Some Windows and Android players need extra software to open certain MOV files.

**Choose MOV when** you are editing on a Mac, working with professional footage or handing files to an editor who asked for it. Convert to MP4 before you share the finished video widely.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | MP4 | WebM | MOV |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Typical codecs | H.264, HEVC | VP9, AV1 | H.264, HEVC, ProRes |
| Works almost everywhere | Yes | Mostly in browsers | Best on Apple devices |
| File size | Small to medium | Often smaller | Can be very large |
| Best for | Sharing, uploading, offline viewing | Websites and web animation | Editing and professional work |

## Which format should you use?

- **Posting to Instagram, TikTok, YouTube or Facebook:** MP4 with H.264 video and AAC audio is the safest choice, and each platform re-encodes the file anyway.
- **Sending a video by message or email:** MP4, because the recipient's device will almost certainly play it.
- **Editing footage from an iPhone or Mac:** keep the original MOV or HEVC file while you work, and export an MP4 when you finish.
- **Embedding a video on your own site:** MP4 works for everyone, and you can add a WebM version for browsers that can use it and save bandwidth.
- **Keeping an offline copy of a video:** MP4, since it is easy to open on any device you own now or later.

## The takeaway

Think of the container as the box and the codec as what is inside it. For almost everyday needs, **MP4** is the right box. Use **WebM** for websites where file size matters, and **MOV** when you are editing on Apple gear. Pick the format for the job, keep your original file, and you will rarely meet a video that will not play.
