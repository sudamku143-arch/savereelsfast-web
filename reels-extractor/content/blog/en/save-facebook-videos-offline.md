Facebook is full of videos worth keeping: a recipe you want to cook from, a talk you want to watch on the train, or a family clip you posted years ago. Facebook makes this harder than it should be, because the obvious button only bookmarks a video. This guide explains what each option really does and how to save a public video as a file.

## "Save video" is not the same as downloading

The **Save video** option in the Facebook app adds a video to your Saved list. That is useful for finding it later, but the video stays on Facebook. If it is deleted or made private, it disappears from your list, and you still need a connection to watch it.

To watch something offline, or to keep it if the post is removed, you need an actual video file on your device.

## Your own videos

If you uploaded the video, you have the easiest options. You can usually download your original from your video settings or from Meta's tools for creators and pages. You can also use your account's "download your information" feature to export a copy of the videos you have shared. Since these are your own files, you do not have to think about anyone else's rights.

## Saving a public video from its link

For a public video you have the right to keep, a browser tool can produce an MP4. The steps are short:

1. Open the video and choose **Share**, then **Copy link**. On a computer, copy the address from the browser bar.
2. Open our [Facebook video downloader](/facebook-video-downloader).
3. Paste the link. The Facebook tab is selected for you.
4. Check the preview and choose **Download**.

You do not need to log in to Facebook for this, and you should never give your Facebook password to a downloader. Short links such as fb.watch also work.

## Which links work

A link works when the video is public. That includes many videos on Pages, Watch and Reels. It does not work for videos shared with friends only, for private groups or for content that has been removed, and no downloader should be able to fetch those. Our guide to [public and friends-only videos](/blog/facebook-video-privacy-what-you-can-save) explains the difference in more detail.

If a link fails, open it in a private window without logging in. If it does not play there, it is not public.

## Quality and sound

Facebook keeps its videos in several qualities. A saved MP4 usually gives you a standard version that plays everywhere, which is fine for watching but not always the highest resolution. Sound normally comes with the video. For the best copy of your own work, use the original you uploaded.

## Be considerate

A video on Facebook belongs to whoever made it. Saving it to watch privately is different from posting it again as if it were yours, which can break copyright and Facebook's rules. Ask before you reuse someone's video, credit them when they agree and remove it if they ask. If you want to understand the legal side better, our article on [downloading videos safely and legally](/blog/download-social-media-videos-safely-and-legally) covers it.

## Troubleshooting

- **"Private or login required":** the video is not public.
- **A link to a photo or a post:** copy the link from the video itself, not from the page around it.
- **A message about being limited:** wait a minute and try again.
- **A live video:** wait until it has ended and been published as a recording.

## Keep your own videos safe

If Facebook is where you publish, keep the source file of every video before you upload it. Export in MP4, since it plays almost everywhere, and store a copy in cloud storage. You will not need any download tool for your own clips, and you will thank yourself if an account is ever locked.
