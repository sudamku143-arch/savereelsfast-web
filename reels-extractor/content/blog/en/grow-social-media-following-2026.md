Growing a following can feel like guesswork, especially when platforms change what they show and a post that worked last month goes nowhere this month. The good news is that the basics have stayed steady. These five tips work on Instagram, TikTok, YouTube and most other platforms, and none of them rely on tricks or shortcuts.

## 1. Pick a niche and say it clearly

People follow accounts that promise something specific. "Videos about my life" is hard to follow, while "quick home workouts for busy parents" is easy. A clear niche helps the platform show your videos to the right viewers and helps a new visitor decide in seconds whether to follow.

To find yours, list three things you know well, three things your audience keeps asking you about, and where those overlap. Put that promise in your bio and your profile picture, and let your first ten videos prove it.

You can always widen your topics later. It is much harder to grow an account that has never said what it is about.

## 2. Hook viewers in the first two seconds

Most people scroll away almost immediately, so the start of a video does most of the work. Begin with the most interesting moment, a clear question or a bold result, instead of a slow introduction.

A few simple ways to open strong:

- **Show the finished result first,** then explain how you got there.
- **Ask a direct question** your viewer wants answered.
- **Use large, readable text** so the video makes sense with the sound off.
- **Cut the dead air.** Remove pauses and filler at the start.

Then deliver on the hook. A promise that the video does not keep may earn a view but not a follower.

## 3. Post consistently, not constantly

Consistency matters more than volume. Posting three good videos a week for six months beats posting twenty a day for one week and then stopping. A steady rhythm gives the platform data about your content and gives your followers a reason to return.

Choose a schedule you can actually keep, then batch your work. Film several videos on one day, edit them on another and schedule them ahead. If you miss a day, simply carry on.

Review your analytics every couple of weeks. Look at watch time, shares and saves rather than likes alone, and make more of what people finish.

## 4. Talk with your audience, not at them

Followers grow when people feel seen. Reply to comments, especially in the first hour after posting, and ask questions that invite an answer. When a comment raises a good question, answer it in a new video.

Take part in your community too. Leave thoughtful comments on other accounts in your niche, work with creators of a similar size and share what others make with credit. Real conversations bring real followers, while mass-following or buying followers brings numbers that never engage, and can hurt how your account performs.

## 5. Reuse your best videos across platforms

A video that did well is proof that an idea works. Instead of leaving it on one platform, adapt it for others. A Reel can become a YouTube Short or a TikTok, with a new caption and hashtags suited to each place, and a long video can be cut into several clips.

## A simple 30-day plan

1. **Week 1:** write your niche in one sentence and update your profile to match.
2. **Week 2:** film and schedule ten videos, each with a strong opening.
3. **Week 3:** post on schedule, reply to every comment and comment on ten accounts in your niche each day.
4. **Week 4:** review your analytics, keep what worked and adapt your best video for a second platform.

## Final thoughts

Growth in 2026 still comes from the same things it always has: a clear focus, a strong start, steady posting, real conversation and smart reuse of your best work. Choose one tip to apply this week, measure what happens and build from there.
