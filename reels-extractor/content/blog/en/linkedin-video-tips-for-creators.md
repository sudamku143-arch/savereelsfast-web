Video on LinkedIn works differently from video on entertainment apps. People are often at a desk or on a commute, they are looking for something useful and they decide within a moment whether a clip is worth their time. This guide collects practical habits for making LinkedIn videos that hold attention, and for keeping your work safe once it is posted.

## Start with one clear point

A video that tries to cover a whole topic usually covers none of it well. Pick a single point: one lesson, one before-and-after, one answer to a question you hear often. If you can say it in one sentence before you record, the video will be easier to follow.

## Make the first seconds count

Most viewers decide quickly. Open with the point itself, or with the problem it solves, instead of a long greeting or a logo animation. A first line such as "Here is the mistake that slows down most launches" tells people what they will get.

## Add captions

Many people watch with the sound off, at work, on a train or in a shared space. Captions let them follow anyway, and they help viewers who are deaf or hard of hearing, or who are not native speakers of your language. Read the captions once before posting, because automatic ones often get names and product terms wrong.

## Keep it short, then earn the extra time

There is no fixed best length, and it depends on the message. A useful rule is to cut anything that does not help the viewer, then stop. A tight 45-second clip is better than a padded three-minute one. Longer formats such as talks and walkthroughs can work well when the audience already wants the detail.

## Choose a shape that fits the feed

Vertical and square videos take up more of a phone screen than wide ones, so they are easier to watch on the go. Wide video still suits screen recordings and recorded talks. Whichever you choose, keep text large enough to read on a small screen and away from the edges.

## Upload the video itself

Posting the video directly to LinkedIn usually works better than sharing a link to it elsewhere, since people can watch without leaving the feed. Add a short text with a question or a takeaway, and reply to comments in the first hours, when the conversation is active.

## Keep your original files

Always keep the file you exported. Save it as MP4, name it with the date and topic, and store it in cloud storage or on a drive you back up. LinkedIn is not a file archive: posts get deleted, company pages change owners and accounts get locked. The exported file is the only copy you fully control.

## Repurpose carefully

A good LinkedIn clip can become a short on another platform, a slide in a training deck or a clip in a newsletter. Go back to your original file for each one, and export it in the right shape rather than re-uploading a downloaded copy.
