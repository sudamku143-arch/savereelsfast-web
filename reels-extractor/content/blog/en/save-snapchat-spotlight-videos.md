Snapchat is designed for things that pass quickly. Snaps vanish, Stories expire and the app makes it hard to keep other people's content, and that is on purpose. Spotlight is the exception: it is a public feed, made for videos to be found and shared. This guide explains what you can save from Snapchat, how to do it and where the limits are.

## Public and private: the one rule

Everything on Snapchat falls into one of two groups:

- **Public content,** such as videos in Spotlight and public profiles. It is meant for anyone to watch.
- **Private content,** such as snaps sent to you, chats and Stories shared with friends only. It is meant for a specific audience.

A downloader without a login can only reach public content, and it should never be used to get around someone's privacy. If a snap was sent to you personally, ask the sender before you save or share it.

## Saving a public Spotlight video

For a public video you have the right to keep, a browser tool can save an MP4. The steps are short:

1. Open the Spotlight video in Snapchat, tap **Share** and choose **Copy link**.
2. Open our [Snapchat video downloader](/snapchat-video-downloader).
3. Paste the link. Short links from t.snapchat.com are accepted too.
4. Check the preview and choose **Download**.

You do not need to log in to Snapchat. Never give your Snapchat username or password to a downloader, since a real tool does not need it.

## What works

- **Public Spotlight videos,** including ones shared by link.
- **Public profile content,** when it is available as a public link.

## What does not work

- **Private snaps and chats.** They are not public and are not available.
- **Friends-only Stories.** Only the intended audience can see them.
- **Deleted or expired content.** Once it is gone, there is nothing to fetch.
- **Links that do not lead to a video.** A profile or a page with no video shows a "no video found" message.

If a link fails, try opening it in a private browser window. If you cannot see the video there, a downloader cannot either.

## Quality and format

Spotlight videos are vertical and short. A saved copy is a standard MP4 that plays on phones, tablets and computers. Quality depends on what Snapchat serves, so it will not be sharper than the original. If the video is yours, the source file from your phone or editor will always be better.

## Keeping your own Snaps and Spotlight videos

Snapchat lets you save your own snaps to Memories, and you can export them to your camera roll. For videos you posted, this is the cleanest way to keep a copy, and it should be your first choice. Back the files up to cloud storage so an account problem never costs you a favourite clip.

## Using saved videos fairly

Watching a public Spotlight video offline is a small, ordinary use. Reposting it, using it in your own videos or putting it in an advertisement is different and needs the creator's permission. Credit them, link to the original and remove the video if they ask. ## Troubleshooting

- **"No video found":** the link points to a profile or a page, not a video.
- **"Private or login required":** the content is not public.
- **A very new video:** wait a minute and try again.
- **A message about limits:** try again shortly.

## Choosing where to post

If you make videos, think about whether a clip suits Stories or Spotlight before you post. We compare them in [Snapchat Spotlight vs Stories](/blog/snapchat-spotlight-vs-stories). Spotlight suits clips that make sense to strangers, and Stories suit updates for people who already follow you.
